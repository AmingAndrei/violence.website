The FLIPSIDE, also erroneously called Hell, is a parallel dimension, and home to all [[daemons|Daemons]].

The FLIPSIDE displays the spatial qualities of a planetary object, although its circumference is mathematically impossible to calculate. Maps are only half-reliable, as locational exactitudes are mostly approximates. The entire dimension displays impossible traits, such as biomes that experience permanent weather or time of day, or even biomes that are impossibly far apart but share the same meteorological and diurnal phenomena, as if they were adjacent to each other. The dimension has a [[hellfire|Hellfire]] Sun (installed by the [[wiki/factions/supreme_acts_of_violence|Supreme Acts of Violence]] to replace the previous one), which appears to somehow orbit it.

Compared to [[sol|Sol]], the FLIPSIDE has a much higher [[anima|Anima]] resonance. The resonance scales upward toward Pandiavolum, where it reaches its peak. This makes [[devils|Devils]], which are at their 'base' strength in the dimension, get exponentially stronger as they get closer to the city.

The FLIPSIDE imposes a negation effect onto all matter which is not native to the dimension, violently destroying any foreign objects that enter, leaving nothing behind. This includes both animate and inanimate objects, and the speed and efficiency of the destruction scale inversely to the size of the object. [[hybrids|Hybrids]] and [[fae|Fae]] half-breeds are almost entirely immune to it, as despite having bodies made mostly of matter from outside of the dimension, the negation effect only strains their bodies, without killing them.

The FLIPSIDE is dimensionally expanding, as the negation effect slowly erodes at the boundaries between itself and that of [[sol|Sol]]. This means that as the FLIPSIDE is increasing in size, the universe [[sol|Sol]] is in shrinks. While this effect was almost completely insignificant in the past, the dimension's expansion accelerated significantly after the arrival of the [[supreme_acts_of_violence|Supreme Acts of Violence]].
