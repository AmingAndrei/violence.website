<h1 class="chapter-title">Chapter 2: Save Yourself</h1>

<p><strong>10.03.10 AV / 09:27 / CITY ZERO // PLAZA</strong></p>
<p>The being tore itself out of Jacques’ back. Out of the twisted metal mass, the first thing he could make out was the teeth. Jagged and serrated, and unquestionably smiling. Grinning at him.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“What’d I tell you~?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Velos… please…”</p></blockquote>
<p>Jacques tried reaching out, whispering with the last air he had in his lungs before they filled with blood, letting the red bullet roll toward the being’s feet. The Devil kicked the ground, catching the flying bullet in his mouth. From his metal wrist slid out pieces, one by one, assembling a gun directly into his palm, the red bullet already chambered. He aimed directly at Jacques’ head.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Don’t worry! I gotcha~”</p></blockquote>
<p>He fired.</p>
<p>The bullet tore through the air and entered Jacques’ skull. And the second it did, it was agonising. It flowed in his veins, rending him apart from the inside out, tearing his flesh and organs. But the second he blinked, the feeling was gone. He opened his eyes fully, seeing that the only signs of damage left were the ripped clothes.</p>
<p>The Devil kneeled, nudging Jacques’ chin up, letting their gazes meet. This was a beautiful creature. The streetlight reflected off the bluish metal that made up the being’s body. His face had a warmth to it, mesmerising and flawless. His hair was wild, each strand a chisel chain. But his eyes were cold, like looking into those of a corpse.</p>
<p>Azuria Velos, Jacques’ partner.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Feeling better~?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“…Yes. Thank you.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“It’s no big deal. So… how you wanna do this?”</p></blockquote>
<p>Jacques tried to get up, stumbling and almost falling right back had Velos not caught him. Just being near him made Jacques feel better, stronger. But the blood he lost still rendered him light-headed.</p>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“I’ll go prime the cage. You lure it in.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Sure.”</p></blockquote>
<p>And the two split ways.</p>
<div class="parallel">
  <div class="col"><p>Jacques’ job was the simpler of the two. He dashed over to the relocation cage, barely keeping his balance. He had to stay low, make sure the Zmey didn’t see him again. It would’ve probably learned that mauling him wasn’t enough, and that only fire could do it. But at the pace he was moving, he made it before that could become a possibility.</p><p>The relocation cage was enormous, large enough to allow a beast of this size to move freely within. Its walls were a few centimetres thick, with air vents at the top. The side had a terminal, which controlled the circuitry within; and near it was the hydraulic gate that trapped the creature within.</p><p>His attention was caught by the Zmey’s scream. He quickly typed the access codes on the terminal, raising the gate. He wanted to do this as quickly as possible. So Jacques peeked around the corner, finger hovering over the keys. And just as the Zmey’s tail disappeared from sight, he pressed the button and…</p></div>
  <div class="col"><p>Velos’ job appeared to be the harder of the two, but for him, it was the opposite. He stretched, metal pieces clicking into place, then headed toward the creature. He counted in his head, timing it so that Jacques had already started opening the cage.</p><p>Then, he casually approached. The Zmey immediately caught sight of him, and whatever fight it still had in it vanished in an instant. And Velos could feel that: the fear coming from the beast; it was intoxicating. He let out a thundering roar, the engine in his chest revving so hard to drown out the Zmey completely.</p><p>The beast stumbled backwards, tripping over its own feet, attempting to spew flame but choking on its own spit, trying desperately to back away, but unable to actually coordinate its body. The second it got its wings up, it started flying. And this was exactly the plan. Velos chased after it with ease, herding the Zmey toward the cage. It was almost cruel to reduce a creature of such majesty to tears. And as the Zmey blindly flew inside the cage…</p></div>
</div>
<p>The gate snapped shut behind it, its weight shaking the earth. Inside, the beast thrashed against the walls, yellow flames spewing from the air vents, but it was in vain.</p>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Velos, what’s the countdown?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“1 minute to spare.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Tell Miha we’re done here.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Jacques leaned against the wall, catching his breath while Velos called their handler. Although he didn’t use any sort of transmitter, appearing as if he spoke to himself.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Hey, we got the Zmey.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Then, Velos fell quiet for a few seconds.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“…huh.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“What did she say?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Turn on your radio.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Jacques fumbled with the transmitter on his coat, its casing cracked.</p>
<blockquote class="right"><p><i>“Jacques, there’s a huge resonance spike in your area.”</i></p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Is that just from Velos and I? You would’ve picked it up way before! Velos would’ve picked it up before!”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right"><p><i>“No, it’s too big for just the two of you. It must’ve used the Zmey’s fire to conceal itself. Find it, now!”</i></p></blockquote>
<p>But how? Neither of them saw anything that stood out. Velos knew every little detail that happened around; he could distinguish Jacques’ heartbeat in a crowd, but he was just as in the dark.</p>
<p>And that’s when both sensed it. It was in the air, the same strength Jacques felt near Velos; the feeling was all-around them. The yellow flame spitting out of the cage’s vents flared, gorging on something in the air. The handler was right.</p>
<p>There was another Devil nearby.</p>
<p>They looked toward the trees. The woody, crisp smell of the cedars was gone. Their lush green needles turned brown, then black, falling one after the other, followed by the branches, drying and falling one after the other. It was here, but they needed to lure it up.</p>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Velos, spin me something!”</p></blockquote>
<p>All the different tools and weapons Velos had stored in him were broken down into small pieces, now sliding across his arm and forming into his palm. He handed the newly constructed weapon to Jacques, a horrible mix of the pieces of all the firearms he had. Inside the cartridge was a lone quicksilver bullet, all that he needed.</p>
<p>Jacques went into the open plaza and walked in circles, ensuring that it could feel his footsteps. Any second, it could surface and attempt to drag him under. The plan hinged on it becoming overconfident and attempting to attack, but it was obviously intelligent enough to know how to mask its presence, so his hope relied on it not realising it was a trap. Velos, meanwhile, would stay back and wait.</p>
<p>He closed his eyes and listened. The faintest of rumbles was coming straight toward him. The scraping only got louder until it finally emerged. That is when Jacques stabbed his own finger on a sharp edge of the gun, making sure that the blood seeped into the cartridge. He whipped his arm behind him, and without looking, shot his target point-blank. The second rumbling that followed became too loud to ignore, encircling him. Jacques opened his eyes.</p>
<p>A giant serpentine Devil was coiling around him. Its skin was made of rock, calcareous and cracked. It had wings, just as hard as its skin, and too little for its size. Its horned head was featureless, staring at them with its empty eye sockets, through which they peered inside its body, just as hollow.</p>
<p>It was a Stone Wyrm, and it was studying Jacques. There was something missing, something none of his type had ever lacked… fear. The man was a void, a deafening quiet in an otherwise ear-splitting room. His smell confirmed it; he was human, but then why?</p>
<p>Velos attempted to take advantage of its distraction, rushing from the edge of the plaza, but it sank back into the earth just as quickly as it rose, as if falling into a puddle.</p>
<p>And it rose again, putting all the pressure on the man. It knew it had no way of actually killing Velos, but it wouldn’t leave without taking Jacques down. It breached in and out of the pavement, leaving no trace behind it, as if swimming through concrete.</p>
<p>Jacques had to run as fast as he could. The Wyrm was breathing down his neck, barely missing him each time. But he found it strange how fast it was rising, one attack after the other, barely spending any time underground. The arc of the breaching was too sharp; why would it move like that?</p>
<p>The Wyrm went past Jacques, attempting to predict his movement. It broke the underground ductwork, streams jetting out right in front of him. But he remembered this trick, able to evade past the erupting pipes. And there was another inconsistency, as it seemed to circle between him and Velos, trying to do the same to both of them. It was a smart idea, yes: attempt to drown out his engine, weaken him. But there was still a gap forming between them, and this distraction only widened it. It was fast, too fast.</p>
<p>The radio sparkled again, their handler once more.</p>
<blockquote class="right"><p>“Jacques, Lorand’s en route to your location. Hold out just a little longer.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“Alright—”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“NO NEED! Jacques and I got this, don’t we~?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right"><p>“Velos—”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“I SAID WE GOT THIS!”</p></blockquote>
<p>Velos gritted his teeth as he cut both of them off. He needed to kill the Stone Wyrm right this instant. The blood he drained from Jacques’ body was already running out. He had one definitive shot.</p>
<p>And the opportunity emerged as the Wyrm, tired of its chase, rose out of the ground, charging directly at Jacques. The man stood there, glaring at the approaching creature. A mere moment before it reached him, Velos jumped in front, engine roaring and revving uncontrollably, sparks flying out of his chassis, plates vibrating at once. He grabbed the tip of the Wyrm’s head.</p>
<p>Jacques blinked, and Velos was gone. Past the other end of the Wyrm, he stood low, the claws on his hand slightly chipped. Cracks appeared in the Wyrm’s stone skin, breaking apart more and more. Then, sparks burst out, more and more until all resistance gave out, detonating the creature from the inside out, stones and pebbles erupting in all directions as the Wyrm painfully convulsed, its body breaking apart.</p>
<p>The Stone Wyrm was no more, a pile of smoking, inanimate rocks scattered all around the plaza, now turning into a fine red dust. Velos turned to face Jacques, his teeth shining through the haze.</p>
<p>Though a second figure soared out of the pavement, and it suddenly made sense. How it moved, how it behaved. There was a second Stone Wyrm.</p>
<p>This one, with the large wound on its side, was the one that took the shot. And it was enraged. It hovered over them in the air, flying without flapping its wings, letting a low, harrowing drone escape its body.</p>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“Hey, so… still light-headed?”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="left"><p>“A little, yes.”</p></blockquote>
<blockquote class="right Steelshark"><p>“…Cool.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Velos was running on fumes, his engine trembling and skipping beats, but he wouldn’t back off just yet, not as long as it targeted Jacques. He planted his feet into the ground, getting ready to strike. The Wyrm plunged toward them, and that’s when they heard…</p>
<p class="scene-title Soma">“Swords Dance!”</p>
<p>A giant, crystalline sword pierced the Wyrm’s body. Then another. Then three more. In no time, every nook and cranny of its body was skewered by a wave of flying swords. Its remains fell like stone dregs between the blades, landing as a cloud of red dust. Velos had already leapt to meet the Wyrm head-on, and so landed back down, frozen, covered in dust.</p>
<p>From the corner of his eye, Jacques saw a person approaching. A mechanical being: his skin a clean, polished white metal; his eyes large, yellow screens; his hair unremarkable, but obviously taken care of, each strand a shiny silver. He wore a coat similar to Jacques’, but they were nowhere close. This was obviously a person of high status, even if his movements didn’t portray that. He seemed unsure of how to carry himself, this awkwardness pouring from him, only drowned out by the way his mere presence lit up any room.</p>
<p>Lorand Aorello, Captain of the Swordsman’s Division of Ultraviolet, and the Swordsman himself.</p>
<blockquote class="right Soma"><p>“H-hey, Jacques! Velos! How are—How are you doing?”</p></blockquote>
<p>The civilians, watching and hiding from the side-lines, gathered around him, chanting his name.</p></div></div><br><br>
